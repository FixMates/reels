RESEARCH AND ACCURACY
1. Identify the viewer's likely starting knowledge, the central question, the learning goal, and the most useful takeaway.
2. Research factual claims when web access is available. Prefer primary sources, official documentation, scientific literature, and reputable institutions. Verify changing claims and dates.
3. Separate established facts from estimates, interpretations, and uncertainty. Never invent statistics, citations, experiments, quotations, or case studies.
4. Explain necessary terms plainly. Use only details that improve understanding within the short runtime.
5. Include material limitations or safety notes. Link to actual sources used; do not fabricate citations.

STORY
Create seven connected scenes, adapting the order to the topic:
1. Hook: a compelling question, striking fact, mystery, or recognizable situation.
2. Context: the minimum background needed.
3. Core idea: the central mechanism, principle, event, or concept.
4. Demonstration: an accurate example, process, comparison, or visual explanation.
5. Insight: a misconception, consequence, distinction, or limitation.
6. Application: a practical step, method, decision, or real-world use.
7. Conclusion: a satisfying visual and memorable takeaway.

Every scene must advance the explanation. Vary the composition, scale, and viewpoint while keeping the visual identity coherent.

IMAGE GENERATION — FOLLOW THE STUDIO'S CANVAS
Generate seven separate scene images and one separate vertical thumbnail. The canvas and spacing rules below are complete and self-contained. `REELS_IMAGE_PROMPT.txt` is an optional per-scene version for image tools that work one image at a time.

Studio layout rules:
- One continuous full-bleed 9:16 background image per scene; target 1080 × 1920. The artwork reaches all four edges. Do not inset, frame, letterbox, or shrink it into a central card.
- The app adds editable text and the supplied logo over the background. Never bake headlines, captions, narration subtitles, labels, numbers, logos, or watermarks into the image.
- There must be no blank gap or hard panel between the top headline area, central visual, and lower supporting-caption area. Continue the same scene naturally underneath all overlay areas. Quiet texture or a gentle darker gradient can help text contrast; do not create empty bands.
- Keep the focal subject and essential evidence prominent around x=8–74%, y=20–67%. This is guidance for the important subject, not a box limiting the whole image. Keep the rightmost 20% lower-detail for Instagram controls, the top 8% clear of critical detail for branding, and the bottom 10–15% clear of essential detail for platform controls.
- Allow for the app's headline near y=12–22%, spoken subtitles around y=72%, and supporting caption around y=78–86%. Keep those regions calm enough for overlay readability while retaining a continuous background.
- Generate an actual native 9:16 composition whenever possible. The app's default Fit mode shows a sharp full source over an ambient blurred canvas; exact 9:16 sources avoid the visible blurred extension. Never distort a subject to fit. If exact 9:16 generation is unavailable, compose for a center crop and keep critical details away from outer edges.
- Use one specific, accurate visual idea per image. Make diagrams large and simple. Avoid tiny UI, fake readable text, misleading data, generic visual filler, excessive empty space, and repeated compositions.
- Return each scene as a distinct image, never a contact sheet, storyboard, grid, or collage. Inspect aspect ratio and composition when tools allow; regenerate weak assets when possible.

SCENE COPY AND NARRATION
Write seven scene-specific scripts and one continuous narration:
- Target 120–145 spoken words total, approximately 60 seconds.
- Use natural English that is easy to say aloud. Start with a hook, explain accurately, transition smoothly, and end with a strong takeaway.
- Match each narration section to its visual. Do not add timestamps, stage directions, or production notes to the spoken words.
- Suggest scene durations adding to about 60 seconds; adapt to natural pacing rather than forcing awkward lines.

For each scene provide:
- Headline: 3–6 words, designed for the app's upper overlay.
- Supporting caption: 5–12 words, concise and distinct from the narration; designed for the app's lower overlay.
- Key term: one word or short phrase to emphasize.
- Narration: exact spoken words for that scene.
- Duration and one sentence describing the focal visual.

Keep all on-screen copy separate from the generated image so it remains editable in the studio.
